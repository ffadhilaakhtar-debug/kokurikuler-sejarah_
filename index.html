Samin People's Struggle

Samin's Struggle

"At the end of the 19th century, in the land of Blora, Central Java, a unique
people's movement was born—the Samin Movement. A form of resistance
against colonialism carried out without violence."

Introduction

The history of Indonesia’s struggle is filled with stories of bravery, sacrifice, and determination to fight against colonialism. However, not all resistance was carried out with weapons. There were also forms of struggle carried out peacefully, with patience, and grounded in strong moral values. One clear example of such a struggle is the movement carried out by the Samin people in Central Java, known as the Samin Movement or Saminism.
The Samin Movement was led by a simple yet highly influential figure named Samin Surosentiko. He opposed Dutch colonialism not through war, but through moral teachings and peaceful actions. Samin taught his followers to remain honest, simple, and to reject all forms of injustice committed by the colonial government. For Samin, the greatest strength of a person does not lie in weapons, but in a pure conscience and the courage to defend the truth.
This paper discusses the struggle of the Samin people against colonialism through non-violent means, starting from the background of the movement, the chronology of important events, its impacts, and the lessons that can be learned.

Values

The teachings of Samin were not only a social movement but also a deep value system. Its core values include honesty (bener), loyalty (setya), simplicity (prasaja), mutual cooperation, independence, and non-violence (tentrem). For the Samin people, resisting does not mean destroying the enemy, but upholding human dignity in a moral way.

Impact

The struggle of the Samin people brought significant impact, both for the followers of the movement, for the Dutch colonial government, and for Indonesia as a whole. For the Samin people themselves, their struggle fostered confidence, dignity, and a spirit of independence. They dared to oppose injustice without using violence. Even though many were imprisoned, monitored, and even treated unfairly by the Dutch, they remained steadfast in their beliefs and in Saminism. For the Dutch colonial government, the Samin Movement was a major challenge. They found it difficult to take action against people who did not commit violence. The Dutch were accustomed to dealing with armed uprisings, but this time, they faced people who resisted with silence and patience. This diminished the authority of the Dutch in the eyes of the people because their power could not overcome the moral strength of common villagers. For Indonesia, the Samin Movement became proof that resistance against colonialism could be conducted peacefully. This movement teaches that courage does not always require war, but also strong resolve to uphold the truth. These values align with the spirit of Indonesia’s independence, which places high importance on justice and peace.
